Experimental algorithms

Algorithms documented in this section have no proof of correctness, and their precision choices are not certified. Their symbols are public but not exported.

Support-cone reduction

Projective hypersurfaces can select the experimental CPU policy with algorithm=:supportcone in DeRham.zeta_coefficients. The policy builds exact plans from the exponent vectors in the defining polynomial's support, sorting those vectors lexicographically. To bound construction cost, it always includes the lexicographically first full-rank subcone when one exists, searches at most the first 256 support-index combinations for more candidates, and retains at most 64. Candidate subcones may overlap and do not form a triangulation. For a chain key it takes the first retained subcone containing the key, uses floored Cramer coordinates for chunk lengths, and lowers the largest length first (lowest index breaks ties) until the plan reaches the terminal pole order. Planned chunks are checked against the frontend's V_S(u,k) predicate. Keys without a valid retained support plan fall back to the depth-first direction rule. An impossible fallback direction raises an error before calling the shared direction chooser.

The support-cone policy preserves the caller's S; the default S=[-1] expands to all ambient indices through the ordinary hypersurface setup. The policy does not support GPU execution, double covers, or custom reduction contexts. It has no proof of correctness, so compare its results with a proven policy such as :depthfirst for the varieties and primes of interest.

AKR pole reduction

Projective hypersurfaces accept three experimental AKR algorithm symbols: :akr, :akr_naive, and :akr_levelbylevel. The first two select the original method, which reduces each monomial's pole-order chain separately. :akr_levelbylevel combines terms at each pole order before applying the reduction map. It reuses the original polynomial-vector conversions for derivatives.

These algorithms are selected with zeta_coefficients(...; algorithm=...). They are not part of the default reduction heuristic.
