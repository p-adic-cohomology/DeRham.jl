Experimental algorithms

Algorithms documented in this section have no proof of correctness, and their precision choices are not certified. Their symbols are public but not exported.

AKR pole reduction

Projective hypersurfaces accept three experimental AKR algorithm symbols: :akr, :akr_naive, and :akr_levelbylevel. The first two select the original method, which reduces each monomial's pole-order chain separately. :akr_levelbylevel combines terms at each pole order before applying the reduction map. Cached UInt64 maps differentiate coefficient vectors. When a cutoff map exceeds the remaining cache budget, both integer representations differentiate shifted monomials directly. Cached BigInt maps and the pseudo-inverse path differentiate polynomials.

These algorithms are selected with zeta_coefficients(...; algorithm=...). They are not part of the default reduction heuristic.

When S contains all variables and the characteristic does not divide the hypersurface degree, AKR builds one Jacobian relation table at degree (n + 1)(d - 2) + 1 and obtains higher-degree relations by multiplying those cutoff relations by monomials. For a proper variable subset or a characteristic dividing the degree, AKR keeps the per-pole-order pseudo-inverse path.

The level-by-level schedule adds input terms already at the terminal pole to the reduced numerator. The catalogue compares both AKR schedules against the default reduction on the fixed Fermat quartic over F₃ with changef=false, termorder=:invlex, and requested relative precision 1. The comparison uses the requested absolute precision of each column.

In earlier runs, entry (21, 1) was 18 modulo 27 with level-by-level AKR and 9 with default and chain-by-chain reduction. The original per-pole-order loop and the earlier cutoff loop both skipped the terminal-pole input. The original full-matrix calculation reached the time limit, leaving its entry unverified.
