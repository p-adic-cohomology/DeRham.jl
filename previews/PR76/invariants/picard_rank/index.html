Picard rank

For a smooth projective surface, the Picard rank is the rank of its group of divisor classes modulo algebraic equivalence. The geometric Picard rank is computed over an algebraic closure; the Picard rank over $\mathbb{F}_{q^k}$ counts classes defined over that finite field.

For a surface in $\mathbb{P}^3$, the hyperplane class is always algebraic. The primitive $H^2$ L-polynomial omits this class, so the ranks returned by the functions below include it.

picard_rank_bound gives an upper bound for the geometric Picard rank; for a general surface this bound need not be exact. The three K3-specific functions give exact geometric or finite-field ranks because the Tate conjecture is known for K3 surfaces over finite fields. They apply to quartic K3 surfaces in $\mathbb{P}^3$: the polynomial form requires a quartic in four variables, and the data form requires 22 primitive-$H^2$ L-polynomial coefficients over $\mathbb{F}_q$. k3_picard_realization_degree is the least extension degree over which the finite-field Picard rank reaches the geometric Picard rank.

DeRham.picard_rank_bound — Function
picard_rank_bound(coeffs::AbstractVector, q)
picard_rank_bound(f::MPolyRingElem; kwargs...)

Geometric upper bound on the Picard rank of a smooth surface in $\mathbb{P}^3$. The data form takes coefficients of its primitive-$H^2$ L-polynomial over $\mathbb{F}_q$ and returns $1$ plus the degree of the cyclotomic part of $L(T/q)$; the added 1 accounts for the hyperplane class. The polynomial form computes those coefficients with zeta_coefficients. This is an upper bound in general and need not be exact. The coefficients may be raw, tate_twist or normalized_tate_twist zeta coefficients.

Arguments

  • coeffs::AbstractVector: primitive-$H^2$ L-polynomial coefficients.
  • f::MPolyRingElem: homogeneous polynomial defining a smooth surface in $\mathbb{P}^3$.
  • q: size of the finite base field for the data form.

Keywords

  • kwargs...: forwarded to zeta_coefficients by the polynomial form.

Returns

An Int giving the geometric upper bound. The polynomial form returns false::Bool when $f$ is not smooth, matching zeta_coefficients.

Examples

The polynomial form calculates the bound for the Fermat quartic over $\mathbb{F}_7$:

julia> f = DeRham.fermat_hypersurface(4, 4, 7);

julia> DeRham.picard_rank_bound(f)
22

If you already have zeta_coefficients output, pass it with the base-field size:

julia> f = DeRham.fermat_hypersurface(4, 4, 7);

julia> coeffs = DeRham.zeta_coefficients(f);

julia> DeRham.picard_rank_bound(coeffs, 7)
22

DeRham.k3_geometric_picard_rank — Function
k3_geometric_picard_rank(coeffs::AbstractVector, q)
k3_geometric_picard_rank(f::MPolyRingElem; kwargs...)

Geometric Picard rank of the quartic K3 surface in $\mathbb{P}^3$ defined by $f$, or given by its primitive-$H^2$ L-polynomial coefficients over $\mathbb{F}_q$. Unlike picard_rank_bound, this is exact because the Tate conjecture is known for K3 surfaces over finite fields. The result includes the hyperplane class. The data form requires 22 coefficients, which may be raw, tate_twist or normalized_tate_twist zeta coefficients. The polynomial form computes the coefficients with zeta_coefficients.

Arguments

  • coeffs::AbstractVector: 22 primitive-$H^2$ L-polynomial coefficients.
  • f::MPolyRingElem: homogeneous quartic in four variables defining a K3 surface in $\mathbb{P}^3$.
  • q: size of the finite base field for the data form.

Keywords

  • kwargs...: forwarded to zeta_coefficients by the polynomial form.

Returns

An Int giving the exact geometric Picard rank. The polynomial form returns false::Bool when $f$ is not smooth; it throws ArgumentError unless $f$ is a quartic in four variables.

Examples

julia> f = DeRham.fermat_hypersurface(4, 4, 7);

julia> DeRham.k3_geometric_picard_rank(f)
22

If you already have zeta_coefficients output, reuse it with $q = 7$:

julia> f = DeRham.fermat_hypersurface(4, 4, 7);

julia> coeffs = DeRham.zeta_coefficients(f);

julia> DeRham.k3_geometric_picard_rank(coeffs, 7)
22

DeRham.k3_picard_rank — Function
k3_picard_rank(coeffs::AbstractVector, q, k)
k3_picard_rank(f::MPolyRingElem, k; kwargs...)

Picard rank of the quartic K3 surface in $\mathbb{P}^3$ defined by $f$, or given by its primitive-$H^2$ L-polynomial coefficients over $\mathbb{F}_q$, after extension to $\mathbb{F}_{q^k}$. The result is exact because the Tate conjecture is known for K3 surfaces over finite fields and includes the hyperplane class. The data form requires 22 coefficients, which may be raw, tate_twist or normalized_tate_twist zeta coefficients. The polynomial form computes the coefficients with zeta_coefficients.

Arguments

  • coeffs::AbstractVector: 22 primitive-$H^2$ L-polynomial coefficients.
  • f::MPolyRingElem: homogeneous quartic in four variables defining a K3 surface in $\mathbb{P}^3$.
  • q: size of the finite base field for the data form.
  • k: positive extension degree.

Keywords

  • kwargs...: forwarded to zeta_coefficients by the polynomial form.

Returns

An Int giving the Picard rank over $\mathbb{F}_{q^k}$. The polynomial form returns false::Bool when $f$ is not smooth; it throws ArgumentError unless $f$ is a quartic in four variables.

Examples

The Fermat quartic over $\mathbb{F}_7$ reaches its geometric rank over $\mathbb{F}_{7^2}$:

julia> f = DeRham.fermat_hypersurface(4, 4, 7);

julia> DeRham.k3_picard_rank(f, 2)
22

If you already have zeta_coefficients output, reuse it with the extension degree:

julia> f = DeRham.fermat_hypersurface(4, 4, 7);

julia> coeffs = DeRham.zeta_coefficients(f);

julia> DeRham.k3_picard_rank(coeffs, 7, 2)
22
