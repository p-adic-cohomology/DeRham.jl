Artin–Mazur height

The Artin–Mazur height of a K3 surface is the height of its one-dimensional formal Brauer group. It is an integer from 1 to 10, or infinity. Height 1 is the ordinary case; infinite height is the supersingular case.

The polynomial form is for a smooth quartic K3 surface in $\mathbb{P}^3$. The data form takes the 22 coefficients of its primitive $H^2$ L-polynomial over $\mathbb{F}_q$; it accepts raw zeta_coefficients output, tate_twist output, or normalized_tate_twist output.

For a quartic in four variables over $\mathbb{F}_p$, the polynomial form calls frobenius_matrix_with_precision(f, [1, 1, 1]) and uses the characteristic polynomial of the returned plain matrix over $\mathbb{Z}/p^M$. It lifts the matrix entries to integers before computing that polynomial; it does not compute the full L-polynomial. Inputs outside this case retain the zeta_coefficients path.

Write the entries returned by coefficients(charpoly(...)) as $c_1,\ldots,c_{22}$. The height test divides $c_i$ by $p^{21-i}$ and checks the result modulo $p$ for $i=2,\ldots,11$. Therefore $c_i$ must be known modulo $p^{22-i}$:

The primitive $H^2$ Hodge numbers are $[1,19,1]$: one column has offset $0$, nineteen have offset $1$, and one has offset $2$. Thus at relative request $[1,1,1]$, column $j$ is known to at least modulus $p^{1+o_j}$, where $o_j$ is its Hodge offset. The returned matrix has one common parent ring $\mathbb{Z}/p^\ell$; its modulus is at least the largest absolute precision, while some columns contain fewer known digits than that common modulus. These column guarantees include the final scaling in FinalReduction.jl.

To see that the tested coefficients meet their required precision, set $k=22-i$. The coefficient $c_i$ is the coefficient of $T^{i-1}$ in $\det(TI-F)$, hence a signed sum of principal minors of size $k$. Mazur divisibility gives every entry in a column of offset $o_j$ valuation at least $o_j$. In the difference between a minor of the exact matrix and the minor of the lifted approximation, each term has at least one error factor from a column $j$; that factor is divisible by $p^{1+o_j}$, and the other factors contribute their column offsets. The error is therefore divisible by $p^{1+\sum_{j\in I}o_j}$ for the selected column set $I$. For $11\leq k\leq20$, the least possible offset sum among $k$ columns is $k-1$ (the offset-$0$ column and $k-1$ of the nineteen offset-$1$ columns). Hence $c_i$ is determined modulo $p^k=p^{22-i}$, exactly the modulus required by the normalized Tate-twist test above. This argument uses only the per-column precision contract of the plain matrix, not a per-column ring or metadata.

For pole order $m$, let $N_m$ be the certified retained-term count, $s_m=m+N_m-1$, and $q_m=2-v_p((p s_m-1)!)$. The computation applies the final column scale $p^{q_m}$ before embedding all columns into one matrix over a common residue ring. The selector and its all-tail proof are documented in the Frobenius matrix precision proof and proved in dr-ine1 / dr-eevm.1. DeRham.artin_mazur_height — Function
artin_mazur_height(coeffs::AbstractVector, q)
artin_mazur_height(f::MPolyRingElem; kwargs...)

Artin–Mazur height of the quartic K3 surface in $\mathbb{P}^3$ defined by $f$, or given by the coefficients of its primitive-$H^2$ L-polynomial over $\mathbb{F}_q$. The data form requires 22 coefficients, which may be raw, tate_twist or normalized_tate_twist zeta coefficients. The polynomial form uses a relative-precision Frobenius matrix for quartics in four variables over a prime field. Other inputs use zeta_coefficients.

Arguments

  • coeffs::AbstractVector: 22 primitive-$H^2$ L-polynomial coefficients.
  • f::MPolyRingElem: homogeneous quartic in four variables defining a K3 surface in $\mathbb{P}^3$.
  • q: size of the finite base field for the data form.

Keywords

  • kwargs...: forwarded to the Frobenius-matrix computation for a prime-field quartic K3, or to zeta_coefficients for other polynomial inputs.

Returns

An Int from 1 to 10, or Inf::Float64 for infinite height. The polynomial form also returns false::Bool when $f$ is not smooth, matching zeta_coefficients.

Examples

The Fermat quartic over $\mathbb{F}_5$ is ordinary and has height 1:

julia> f = DeRham.fermat_hypersurface(4, 4, 5);

julia> DeRham.artin_mazur_height(f)
1

If you already have zeta_coefficients output, reuse it with $q = 5$:

julia> f = DeRham.fermat_hypersurface(4, 4, 5);

julia> coeffs = DeRham.zeta_coefficients(f);

julia> DeRham.artin_mazur_height(coeffs, 5)
1

The Fermat quartic over $\mathbb{F}_7$ is supersingular and has infinite height:

julia> f = DeRham.fermat_hypersurface(4, 4, 7);

julia> DeRham.artin_mazur_height(f)
Inf

artin_mazur_height(f; kwargs...)

artin_mazur_height for the quartic K3 surface in $\mathbb{P}^3$ defined by the homogeneous polynomial $f$. For a quartic in four variables over a prime field, this requests relative precision $[1, 1, 1]$ from frobenius_matrix_with_precision, computes the characteristic polynomial after lifting entries of its plain matrix over $\mathbb{Z}/p^M$ to $\mathbb{Z}$, and tests the coefficient residues needed for the height. The matrix has one common parent ring; its columns are known to the absolute precisions implied by their Hodge offsets and the requested relative precision. Other inputs use zeta_coefficients and the coefficient method. Returns false when $f$ is not smooth, matching zeta_coefficients.
