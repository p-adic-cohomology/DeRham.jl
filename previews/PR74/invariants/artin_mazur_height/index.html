Artin–Mazur height

The Artin–Mazur height of a K3 surface is the height of its one-dimensional formal Brauer group. It is an integer from 1 to 10, or infinity. Height 1 is the ordinary case; infinite height is the supersingular case.

The polynomial form is for a smooth quartic K3 surface in $\mathbb{P}^3$. The data form takes the 22 coefficients of its primitive $H^2$ L-polynomial over $\mathbb{F}_q$; it accepts raw zeta_coefficients output, tate_twist output, or normalized_tate_twist output.

DeRham.artin_mazur_height — Function
artin_mazur_height(coeffs::AbstractVector, q)
artin_mazur_height(f::MPolyRingElem; kwargs...)

Artin–Mazur height of the quartic K3 surface in $\mathbb{P}^3$ defined by $f$, or given by the coefficients of its primitive-$H^2$ L-polynomial over $\mathbb{F}_q$. The data form requires 22 coefficients, which may be raw, tate_twist or normalized_tate_twist zeta coefficients. The polynomial form computes them with zeta_coefficients.

Arguments

  • coeffs::AbstractVector: 22 primitive-$H^2$ L-polynomial coefficients.
  • f::MPolyRingElem: homogeneous quartic in four variables defining a K3 surface in $\mathbb{P}^3$.
  • q: size of the finite base field for the data form.

Keywords

  • kwargs...: forwarded to zeta_coefficients by the polynomial form.

Returns

An Int from 1 to 10, or Inf::Float64 for infinite height. The polynomial form also returns false::Bool when $f$ is not smooth, matching zeta_coefficients.

Examples

The Fermat quartic over $\mathbb{F}_5$ is ordinary and has height 1:

julia> f = DeRham.fermat_hypersurface(4, 4, 5);

julia> DeRham.artin_mazur_height(f)
1

If you already have zeta_coefficients output, reuse it with $q = 5$:

julia> f = DeRham.fermat_hypersurface(4, 4, 5);

julia> coeffs = DeRham.zeta_coefficients(f);

julia> DeRham.artin_mazur_height(coeffs, 5)
1

The Fermat quartic over $\mathbb{F}_7$ is supersingular and has infinite height:

julia> f = DeRham.fermat_hypersurface(4, 4, 7);

julia> DeRham.artin_mazur_height(f)
Inf

artin_mazur_height for the quartic K3 surface in $\mathbb{P}^3$ defined by the homogeneous polynomial $f$, computed by calling zeta_coefficients(f; kwargs...) (with $q$ the characteristic of parent(f)) and delegating to the coefficient method. Returns false when $f$ is not smooth, matching zeta_coefficients.
